# Escape from Jerusalem

> **An interactive Book of Mormon Adventure.**

## The Game

**Escape from Jerusalem** is a mobile-first, 2D top-down puzzle/adventure game inspired by *The Book of Mormon*.

The player explores Jerusalem, interacts with its people and environment, solves puzzles, and helps Lehi's family escape the city.

After reaching the wilderness, the family's journey takes an unexpected turn: they must return to Jerusalem to obtain the brass plates from Laban before escaping once again.

The initial game is a focused, approximately **one-hour adventure based primarily on 1 Nephi 1–4**.

---

## The Player Experience

The player should feel like they are **discovering a place and uncovering a story**, rather than simply following a sequence of objectives.

The core experience is:

**Explore → Investigate → Interact → Solve**

Players explore a compact, interconnected Jerusalem; talk with NPCs; examine their surroundings; discover clues; solve environmental puzzles; and gradually understand what is happening to Lehi's family.

Previously visited locations become meaningful again when the player returns to Jerusalem.

---

## The Hook

The player begins the adventure believing they understand who they are and what their journey will be.

Then the story changes.

The player discovers that escaping Jerusalem was only the beginning.

---

## Narrative Spine

**Jerusalem → Escape → Wilderness → Tents → Return to Jerusalem → Brass Plates → Escape Again**

The first escape establishes the player's initial understanding of the adventure.

The wilderness changes the direction of the story.

The return to Jerusalem provides the game's major adventure.

The final escape allows the player to experience familiar locations from a new perspective.

---

## Design Pillars

### 1. Exploration

Jerusalem should feel like a place the player can learn, navigate, and become familiar with.

### 2. Discovery

Story and gameplay information should be discovered through NPCs, objects, environments, clues, and exploration.

### 3. Puzzle Adventure

Environmental and information-based puzzles should give the player meaningful problems to solve without making combat the central mechanic.

### 4. Narrative Perspective

The player's understanding of the story should evolve as they discover new information. The game should use the player's limited perspective to create surprise, discovery, and narrative payoff.

### 5. Faithful Adaptation

Major events from *1 Nephi 1–4* should remain faithful to the source. Gameplay may expand the spaces between documented events without contradicting them.

---

## Initial Scope

The first complete version should contain:

* **2D top-down gameplay**
* **Mobile-first design**
* **Godot**
* **Approximately one hour of gameplay**
* **One interconnected Jerusalem adventure**
* **Wilderness transition**
* **NPC interaction**
* **Environmental exploration**
* **Environmental and information-based puzzles**
* **Return to previously visited areas**
* **The brass plates objective**
* **Final escape from Jerusalem**

Combat is optional and secondary. It should never replace the exploration, puzzle-solving, and narrative experience that defines the game.

---

## The World

The game focuses on a compact, gameplay-oriented representation of **Jerusalem around 600 BC**.

The city should contain interconnected areas such as:

* Residential districts
* Marketplace
* Temple area
* Wealthier districts
* Narrow streets
* City gates
* Family residence
* Laban's area
* Hidden or restricted areas
* Wilderness outskirts

The goal is not to recreate the entire historical city.

The goal is to create a **small world worth learning**.

---

## Success

The game succeeds if a player can:

1. Become immersed in Jerusalem.
2. Understand the basic gameplay without extensive instruction.
3. Enjoy exploring and solving problems.
4. Become invested in Lehi's family's situation.
5. Develop an understanding of the characters and their relationships.
6. Become invested in the family's journey.
7. Experience meaningful gameplay during the return to Jerusalem.
8. Escape Jerusalem again.
9. Finish the game feeling that they experienced a complete adventure—not merely a prototype.

---

## North Star

When deciding whether something belongs in the game, ask:

> **Does this make the player's journey through Jerusalem more interesting, meaningful, or fun?**

If it does, it may belong.

If it does not, it should probably wait.

The initial goal is **not to build the entire Book of Mormon**.

The goal is to build **one great adventure**:

> **You thought you escaped Jerusalem.**
>
> **Now go back. Get the plates. Escape again.**

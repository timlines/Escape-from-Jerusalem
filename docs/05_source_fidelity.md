# Escape from Jerusalem

## Source Fidelity

> **The Book of Mormon is the primary narrative source for the initial adventure.**

---

# 1. Purpose

This document establishes the rules for adapting the *Book of Mormon* into an interactive game.

The goal is to preserve the major narrative events, characters, themes, and scriptural meaning while allowing enough creative freedom to create a compelling game.

The game should be an **adaptation**, not a literal recreation of every verse.

---

# 2. Primary Source

The primary source material for the initial adventure is:

* **1 Nephi 1**
* **1 Nephi 2**
* **1 Nephi 3**
* **1 Nephi 4**

These chapters provide the primary narrative framework for the initial game.

Other scripture may be consulted for:

* Historical context
* Character information
* Geographic context
* Cultural context
* Clarification of terminology
* Later references to the events

However, the initial game should remain focused on the events surrounding Lehi's departure from Jerusalem and the acquisition of the brass plates.

---

# 3. Core Narrative Events

The following major events should be preserved.

## 1 Nephi 1

### Lehi's Visions

Lehi receives visions concerning:

* The destruction of Jerusalem
* The coming of the Messiah
* The future of his people

These events establish the spiritual and narrative foundation of the story.

---

## Lehi Preaches in Jerusalem

Lehi preaches to the people of Jerusalem.

His message is rejected by many.

This creates the growing danger surrounding Lehi and his family.

The game may expand the surrounding environment and interactions to help the player understand the social context.

---

# 4. 1 Nephi 2 — Departure

Lehi receives a commandment to depart from Jerusalem.

The family leaves:

**Jerusalem → Wilderness**

The game should communicate that this is not simply a voluntary relocation.

It is an act of faith and obedience.

The departure from Jerusalem is the conclusion of the game's opening act.

---

# 5. 1 Nephi 3 — Return for the Brass Plates

Lehi commands his sons to return to Jerusalem.

The purpose is to obtain the brass plates from Laban.

This establishes the central objective of the initial adventure.

The brothers return to Jerusalem.

They attempt to obtain the plates.

---

# 6. The First Attempt

The brothers cast lots to determine who will approach Laban.

**Laman is selected.**

Laman approaches Laban and requests the records.

Laban refuses.

The brothers then attempt to exchange their family's wealth for the plates.

Laban takes their property but does not surrender the records.

The brothers flee.

These events should remain recognizable in the game even if the player experiences them through interactive gameplay rather than passive cutscenes.

---

# 7. The Second Attempt

The brothers return to recover their property.

They are discovered.

Laban's servants pursue them.

The brothers escape.

The brothers become discouraged and angry.

Nephi proposes another approach.

These events establish the increasing tension before the final attempt.

---

# 8. Nephi's Response

Nephi expresses his willingness to obey the commandment.

His response is one of the defining moments of the narrative.

The game should preserve the importance of Nephi's faith and determination.

The player should experience Nephi as an important character rather than simply as another member of the party.

---

# 9. 1 Nephi 4 — Nephi Enters Jerusalem

Nephi enters Jerusalem alone.

He is guided by the Spirit.

He encounters Laban.

The events surrounding Laban and the brass plates form the narrative climax of the initial mission.

The player does **not** become Nephi.

The player remains Laman and experiences Nephi's actions from the perspective of the other brothers.

This distinction is a core design feature.

---

# 10. The Laman Reveal

### Internal Design Requirement

The player character is **Laman**.

However, the player should initially believe they are controlling **Nephi**.

This information must be protected in player-facing material.

The reveal should occur naturally when Lehi commands his sons to return to Jerusalem.

Lehi addresses the player:

> **“Laman, behold, I would that thou shouldst go again unto the land of Jerusalem...”**

Nephi then responds:

> **“I will go and do the things which the Lord hath commanded...”**

This establishes that:

1. The player is Laman.
2. Nephi is a separate character.
3. The player's understanding of the first act was incomplete.
4. The Brass Plates mission will be experienced from Laman's perspective.

### Spoiler Protection

The identity of the player character should not be revealed through:

* Marketing
* Store descriptions
* Trailers
* Public pitch materials
* Screenshots
* UI
* Character selection
* Narration
* Internal monologue
* Developer-facing material that could accidentally become player-facing

The reveal should be **experienced by the player rather than explained to them beforehand.**

---

# 11. Scriptural Dialogue

Direct scripture should be treated as authoritative source material.

When dialogue is taken directly from scripture:

* The wording should be verified against the source.
* The correct chapter and verse should be recorded.
* Quotations should not be casually paraphrased when direct quotation is intended.
* Context should be preserved.

Any scriptural quotation used in the final game should undergo a final source verification pass.

---

# 12. Creative Additions

The game requires material that is not explicitly described in scripture.

Creative additions are permitted when they support gameplay and atmosphere.

Examples include:

* NPCs
* Civilian characters
* Shops
* Markets
* Streets
* Buildings
* Environmental puzzles
* Exploration
* Optional conversations
* Travel sequences
* Schedules
* Daily routines
* Environmental storytelling
* Alternate routes
* Gameplay objectives
* Transitional scenes

These additions should make the world feel alive without contradicting the source.

---

# 13. What Creative Additions Must Not Do

Creative additions should not:

* Contradict major scriptural events.
* Change the identity of major characters.
* Reverse important narrative outcomes.
* Change the purpose of the brass plates mission.
* Remove the central role of Lehi's commandments.
* Turn Nephi into an antagonist.
* Make Laman secretly responsible for events attributed to another character.
* Present invented events as direct scripture.
* Undermine the central themes of faith, family, obedience, and survival.

---

# 14. Gameplay vs. Scripture

Gameplay may require the player to perform actions that are not explicitly described in scripture.

This is acceptable.

For example, the player may:

* Walk through areas not described in the text.
* Speak with fictional inhabitants.
* Solve environmental puzzles.
* Investigate locations.
* Observe NPC schedules.
* Choose routes through Jerusalem.
* Discover optional information.
* Complete transitional objectives.

These activities exist to allow the player to **experience the world between documented events**.

The game should distinguish between:

> **What scripture says happened**

and:

> **What the player does to experience those events interactively.**

---

# 15. Historical Representation

The game takes place in Jerusalem around **600 BC**.

Historical research may be used to inform:

* Architecture
* Clothing
* Geography
* Trade
* Food
* Daily life
* Social structures
* Religious practices
* Tools
* Transportation
* Urban organization

Historical details should be treated as supporting research rather than replacing the primary narrative source.

When historical certainty is low, the game should favor:

1. Scriptural consistency
2. Reasonable historical plausibility
3. Gameplay clarity

---

# 16. Character Fidelity

Major characters should remain recognizable in their scriptural roles.

### Lehi

A prophet and father whose revelations and commandments drive the family's journey.

### Sariah

Lehi's wife and mother of the family.

### Laman

The oldest son and player character.

His perspective should be treated with care rather than turning him into a caricature of opposition.

### Lemuel

Laman's brother and a source of family conflict and interaction.

### Sam

A supportive member of the family.

### Nephi

A younger brother whose faith and determination become increasingly important.

### Laban

Keeper of the brass plates and the primary opposing force in the Brass Plates mission.

---

# 17. Thematic Fidelity

The adaptation should preserve the major themes present in the source material.

Important themes include:

* Faith
* Obedience
* Family
* Sacrifice
* Revelation
* Perseverance
* Fear
* Uncertainty
* Conflict
* Survival
* Trust in God

The game should not require the player to already understand these themes.

They should emerge naturally through the player's experience.

---

# 18. Player Choice and Canon

The game may provide multiple ways for the player to approach problems.

However, player choice should not change the major canonical outcome of the initial adventure.

The player may choose:

* Where to go
* Who to talk to
* What information to pursue
* Which route to take
* How to approach certain obstacles
* Which optional discoveries to make

The player should not be able to fundamentally rewrite the major events established by scripture.

The design goal is:

> **Freedom within the story, not freedom from the story.**

---

# 19. Canonical Events vs. Gameplay Events

When designing a sequence, classify events as either:

### Canonical

Events explicitly established by scripture.

These should remain faithful.

### Supporting

Events created to connect canonical events.

These may be creatively designed as long as they remain consistent with the source.

### Optional

Events that enrich exploration but are not required for the main story.

These should be especially careful not to contradict the source.

---

# 20. Source Verification Process

Before finalizing major narrative content:

1. Identify the relevant scripture.
2. Record the chapter and verse.
3. Verify the event or quotation.
4. Determine what is explicitly stated.
5. Determine what is inferred.
6. Identify what must be invented for gameplay.
7. Ensure invented material does not contradict the source.
8. Perform a final scriptural review before release.

This process should be applied especially to:

* Major dialogue
* Major story events
* Character actions
* Scripture quotations
* The Brass Plates sequence
* The Laman reveal
* Nephi's encounter with Laban

---

# 21. Adaptation Rule

The central adaptation rule is:

> **Be faithful to what happened. Be creative about what the player does between those events.**

The game does not need to turn every verse into a cutscene.

Instead, scripture provides the **narrative spine** while gameplay provides the **experience between the bones of the story**.

---

# 22. Final Standard

When evaluating a proposed addition, ask:

### Does it contradict scripture?

If yes:

**Do not include it.**

### Does it support the story?

If yes:

**Consider including it.**

### Does it make the game more interactive?

If yes:

**Consider including it.**

### Does it make the world more believable?

If yes:

**Consider including it.**

### Does it exist only because it sounds cool?

If yes:

**It probably belongs in the future pile.**

---

# Source Fidelity North Star

> **Scripture provides the story.**
>
> **The player provides the experience.**
>
> **Gameplay fills the space between the events without changing what happened.**

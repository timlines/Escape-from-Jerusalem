# Escape-from-Jerusalem

> A 2D top-down puzzle/adventure game inspired by the Book of Mormon, following Laman's perspective during the family's departure from Jerusalem and the return to obtain the brass plates.

---

## 🎮 Project Overview

**Escape from Jerusalem** is a mobile-first, 2D top-down puzzle/adventure game being developed in **Godot**.

The game draws its primary narrative source from **The Book of Mormon**, particularly **1 Nephi 1–4**.

The visual and gameplay inspiration is **The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past**, with an emphasis on exploration, environmental puzzles, character interaction, discovery, and adventure.

The game is designed as an approximately **one-hour experience** centered around a single major adventure:

> **Laman and his brothers must leave Jerusalem, return to Jerusalem to obtain the brass plates, and escape once again.**

A major narrative twist is that the player initially believes they are playing **Nephi**, but does not discover that they are actually playing **Laman** until Lehi commands his sons to return to Jerusalem.

---

# 🎯 Core Vision

The goal is to create a game that is:

* Fun first
* Faithful to the Book of Mormon's major narrative events
* Accessible to mobile players
* Easy to learn
* Rich in exploration and environmental storytelling
* Appropriate for the intended 14–18 audience
* Expandable into a larger game universe
* Capable of supporting future Ward Radio-related content
* Built with reusable original assets that may have future merchandising applications

The initial release should be treated as a **focused vertical slice / first complete adventure**, rather than attempting to build the entire Book of Mormon narrative.

# 📖 Story

## Narrative Premise

The game begins in **Jerusalem**.

The player controls an unnamed young man.

The game deliberately avoids revealing his identity.

Because Nephi is the best-known character associated with this story, the player is expected to naturally assume that they are playing **Nephi**.

They are not.

The player is actually **Laman**, Lehi's oldest son.

The identity is revealed naturally during the story when Lehi commands his sons to return to Jerusalem to obtain the brass plates.

Lehi addresses the player:

> **“Laman, behold, I would that thou shouldst go again unto the land of Jerusalem...”**

The player realizes:

> **“I'm Laman?”**

Immediately afterward, Nephi responds:

> **“I will go and do the things which the Lord hath commanded...”**

The player now understands that Nephi is a separate character.

The game then continues from Laman's perspective.

---

# 🧩 Narrative Structure

## Act I — Jerusalem

### Purpose

Introduce:

* The player
* Jerusalem
* Lehi
* Lehi's family
* Basic controls
* Exploration
* Environmental interaction
* The growing danger surrounding Lehi

### Player Knowledge

The player does **not** know they are Laman.

The game should avoid:

* Naming the protagonist
* Nameplates identifying the protagonist
* Narration identifying the protagonist
* Dialogue directly addressing the protagonist by name

### Gameplay

The player explores a portion of Jerusalem.

Possible activities include:

* Exploring streets
* Entering buildings
* Speaking with NPCs
* Examining objects
* Solving simple environmental puzzles
* Learning about Lehi's preaching
* Discovering the growing hostility toward Lehi

---

## Act I Finale — Leaving Jerusalem

Lehi receives a commandment from the Lord to take his family and depart into the wilderness.

The family prepares to leave.

The player participates in the preparations.

The family leaves Jerusalem.

The player believes they have accomplished the game's primary objective.

### The player has escaped Jerusalem.

**Act I ends.**

---

# Act II — The Wilderness

The family establishes itself in the wilderness.

The player begins learning that the journey is not over.

Lehi receives another commandment.

His sons must return to Jerusalem to obtain the **brass plates from Laban**.

The brothers gather.

Lehi addresses his oldest son.

---

# ⭐ THE IDENTITY REVEAL

Lehi speaks to the player:

> **“Laman, behold, I would that thou shouldst go again unto the land of Jerusalem...”**

The player finally learns their identity.

### The player is Laman.

This should be presented as a major narrative moment.

The player has spent the entire first act assuming they were Nephi.

Immediately afterward, Nephi responds:

> **“I will go and do the things which the Lord hath commanded...”**

The player now realizes:

**Nephi was never the player character.**

---

# Act III — Return to Jerusalem

The brothers return to Jerusalem.

The player now knows they are Laman.

The city is familiar because the player has already explored portions of it.

This creates an opportunity to reuse locations while giving them new gameplay purposes.

The player's objective is now:

## Obtain the Brass Plates

---

# The First Attempt

The brothers attempt to obtain the brass plates from Laban.

The brothers determine who will approach Laban.

Laman is selected.

Laman approaches Laban and requests the records.

Laban refuses.

The brothers attempt to exchange their family's wealth for the records.

Laban takes their property but refuses to surrender the plates.

The brothers flee.

---

# The Second Attempt

The brothers return to recover their property.

They are discovered.

Laban's servants pursue them.

The brothers escape.

The situation becomes increasingly desperate.

Laman and Lemuel become angry with Nephi.

Nephi proposes another plan.

---

# Act IV — Nephi

Nephi decides to enter Jerusalem alone.

The player remains Laman.

Nephi becomes the active character within the story, but the player does not take control of him.

Nephi is led by the Spirit.

He encounters Laban.

The events described in **1 Nephi 4** take place.

This is the narrative climax.

The player experiences this portion of the story from Laman's perspective.

---

# Act V — Escape

Nephi returns with the brass plates.

The brothers now possess the record they were commanded to obtain.

The objective changes:

> **Escape Jerusalem.**

The player controls Laman.

The final sequence takes the player through Jerusalem and back toward the wilderness.

Previously visited locations can be revisited.

The player now sees the city differently.

The journey culminates with the brothers successfully returning to their family.

---

# Ending

The family prepares to continue its journey.

The brass plates have been obtained.

Jerusalem is behind them.

The wilderness lies ahead.

The screen fades to black.

A scripture-based closing line appears:

> **“And we did again take our journey in the wilderness...”**

## ESCAPE FROM JERUSALEM

# 🎮 Core Gameplay Loop

The core gameplay loop is:

**Explore → Discover → Interact → Solve → Progress → Explore**

The player should spend most of the game:

* Exploring environments
* Discovering information
* Talking with NPCs
* Solving environmental puzzles
* Finding paths through Jerusalem
* Completing objectives
* Making progress toward escape

Combat, if included, should support the adventure rather than become the central gameplay mechanic.

---

# 🧩 Puzzle Design

Puzzle design should be inspired by classic Zelda-style environmental puzzles.

Potential mechanics:

### Environmental

* Pushable objects
* Switches
* Gates
* Hidden passages
* Keys
* Locked buildings
* Pressure plates
* Water/environment manipulation
* Light/dark interactions
* Multi-step environmental puzzles

### Information-Based

* NPC clues
* Written clues
* Symbols
* Maps
* Environmental storytelling
* Remembering locations
* Connecting information discovered in different areas

### Exploration

* Finding alternate routes
* Discovering hidden areas
* Returning to previously visited locations
* Unlocking shortcuts

The first act should introduce these mechanics gradually.

The final act should combine them into more complex challenges.

---

# 🗺️ World Design

The initial game should focus on a relatively compact section of:

## Jerusalem

Rather than attempting to recreate the entire historical city, the game should create a **gameplay-focused representation**.

Potential areas:

* Residential district
* Marketplace
* Temple area
* Wealthy district
* Narrow streets
* City gates
* Family residence
* Laban's area
* Hidden/secret areas
* Wilderness outskirts

The map should encourage the player to learn the environment.

The return to Jerusalem in Act III should make use of previously visited areas.

# 🎨 Art Direction

## Primary Style

**2D top-down pixel/illustrated adventure style**

Primary inspiration:

### The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past

The game should aim for:

* Strong readable silhouettes
* Simple environments
* Distinctive characters
* Clear interactive objects
* Visually understandable puzzles
* Compact environments
* Mobile-friendly readability

The game should develop its **own visual identity** rather than directly reproducing Zelda's art.

---

# 📱 Mobile Design

The game is designed for mobile from the beginning.

Controls should prioritize:

* Touch movement
* Large interactive buttons
* Minimal UI clutter
* Clear interaction prompts
* Readable text
* Short gameplay interactions
* Portrait/landscape decision to be determined early

The game should not simply be a desktop game shrunk onto a phone.

Mobile interaction should influence the design from the beginning.

# 📜 Source-Fidelity Policy

The **Book of Mormon is the primary narrative source**.

The principal source material is:

* **1 Nephi 1**
* **1 Nephi 2**
* **1 Nephi 3**
* **1 Nephi 4**

Scriptural events should not be changed merely for convenience.

Creative additions are permitted for gameplay, including:

* Exploration
* Puzzles
* NPC interactions
* Environmental storytelling
* Travel sequences
* Gameplay objectives
* Transitions between documented events

However:

> **Creative additions should not contradict the primary source.**

Direct scripture should be quoted accurately.

Scriptural dialogue should be verified against the source before being finalized.
